Today I had a video call with my mentor about where to send the paper. Before the call, my idea of publishing was simple: finish the paper, pick a journal and send it. Most of the call was about everything in between, and I took more notes than in any meeting so far.

The first question was the journal. Several of the papers closest to mine were published in Sensors, so that was where I had been looking. My mentor thought we should aim higher than MDPI journals. He suggested Biomedical Signal Processing and Control, an Elsevier journal he has submitted to before. I hadn't really understood impact factors until then. An impact factor is roughly how often a journal's recent papers get cited, and people use it as a rough way to rank journals. It doesn't tell you whether a single paper is good, but it does affect how the paper is seen.

After the call I read the journal's guide for authors. A few of its rules changed my plans:

- The paper should be about 5,000 words. Mine is around 5,500 to 6,000 without the references, so I'll have to cut 10 to 20 percent. My mentor said to wait for his comments before cutting, so I don't cut the wrong parts.
- The abstract can be at most 250 words.
- The journal requires "highlights," which are 3 to 5 bullet points of 85 characters or fewer. After a week of turning my bullet points into paragraphs, I found this a little funny.
- The data behind the paper has to be in a public repository. My mentor agreed that I should release the code, so the plan is to put the code on GitHub and the result files on Zenodo, which gives them a DOI.
- The first decision usually takes two to four months, and a paper can only be under review at one journal at a time. If it gets rejected, those months are gone before I can try somewhere else.

There were two things I had never heard of. One is arXiv, where researchers post papers publicly before or during review. The first time you post there, you need an endorsement from someone who has posted before, and my mentor said he would endorse me. The other is a conference short paper. He suggested also writing a 4-page paper for a conference, but it can't be a shortened copy of the journal paper. It has to focus on one question or look at the work from a different angle, and the two papers have to cite each other and mention each other when they are submitted. We haven't picked the conference yet.

He also said no to my title. I had proposed "Body Coverage Matters More Than Sensor Density," and he said it sounded like a blog post. I see what he means, since it states the conclusion like a headline. For now I'm using a plainer one that starts with "Effect of Sensor Density and Body Coverage on Pressure-Mat Sleep-Posture Classification." It's less exciting, but it says what the paper measures.

The plan is to send him the full draft tomorrow with the red notes filled in, go through it together at a meeting on the 28th, and then cut it down and prepare everything else. If that goes well, it could be submitted and posted on arXiv in the first half of October. The schedule is tight, and most of tomorrow will be spent copying exact numbers and settings into the parts that are still red.
